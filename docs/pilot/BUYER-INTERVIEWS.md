# Five setup-pilot interviews

Prepared 17 October 2026. This is a research script and service-scope hypothesis, not a published offer, contract, quote or validated price. It implements the assisted-setup experiment in the existing revenue working proposal. Keep the public PDF tools free, open source and on-device.

## Who and how

Speak with five people responsible for a small agency's administrative workflows or static-site decisions. A tester can introduce a decision-maker only with permission. Use owner-approved contacts/channels; record actual outreach privately. Do not imply they are buyers until they express a concrete need. No customer files are needed.

> I maintain FolioCove, the free open-source browser PDF workspace. I am exploring whether small teams need help deploying the existing tools on infrastructure they control. Could I ask about your current PDF process for 20 minutes? This is research; you can use and self-host the source without buying a service. Please do not share client documents or credentials.

## Twenty-minute interview

Ask about their existing workflow before introducing the proposed service:

1. “Walk me through the last time your team prepared a PDF pack. What triggered it? Who did what?”
2. “What tool did you use, how often does this arise, and what took the most time or caused mistakes?” Ask for a recent concrete example, without the document or client details.
3. “Have you tried changing that process or paid for help before? What happened?” Include doing nothing as an alternative.
4. “Who chooses the tool, who could approve spending, and what would need to happen before adopting a browser utility?” Ask whether current browsers, internal approval or fidelity requirements rule it out.
5. “Would you prefer the free public app, deploying the source yourselves, or getting deployment help? Why?” Do not manufacture a need for hosting if the public tool suffices.

Then describe the scope below. Ask: “For that exact scope, would a one-time experimental ₹4,999 setup fee solve a current problem? What would prevent it?” Price approval alone is weak evidence. If the need is current, ask whether they want a written scope, who should review it and when a decision is realistic. Log a concrete next step or a clear rejection. A request for a quote is not revenue.

## Proposed setup scope for a written quote

| Included hypothesis | Acceptance / boundary |
| --- | --- |
| One agreed existing FolioCove version on one compatible customer-owned static host. | Customer controls its account and hosting bill; retain MIT and third-party license notices. Record the exact version. |
| Configure the agreed base path and ordinary static delivery. | Verify the chosen hosted path and local dependency assets. No enterprise authentication, cloud storage or new backend. |
| Synthetic merge and extraction acceptance check. | Merge the existing two samples into three correctly ordered labeled pages; extract pages 2–4 from the six-page sample into one three-page PDF; download and open both on the agreed tested desktop browser. |
| Written handover for deployment, version, known limits and support contact. | Agree the correction window and communication channel in writing before accepting work. No ongoing maintenance or response-time promise included. |

Keep a minimal private buyer ledger using [the privacy instructions](RESULTS.md): buyer code, role group, recent job/pain, current alternative, need urgency, decision authority, offer outcome, objection, agreed next action, scope/quote state, payment state, actual delivery hours and direct costs. Use codes in any analysis; contracts, payments and contacts stay out of public source/history.

After five conversations, report exact counts and distinguish unprompted recurring needs from prompted reactions. One paid pilot requires an actual accepted scope and received payment; call it delivered only after the synthetic acceptance check and handover. Track gross receipts, costs and time separately. After three delivered pilots, review whether the fee covers the work before proposing maintenance or adding paid features. If the public app already solves the need, invite its use and record that evidence instead of forcing a service sale.
